## Welcome Event

First up was the welcome event on Friday evening, which took place at a rooftop
bar overlooking Jakominiplatz; this was lovely in the evening light, and
spectacular at night with parts of the city lit up!

I had fun helping to prepare and hand out lanyards to everyone as they arrived.
This was also where Nico introduced many of us to Sturm — young, lightly
fermented wine; sweet and flavorful. This became a hit at the welcome event and
indeed all of Akademy!

## Conference Talks

Saturday and Sunday saw a deluge of great talks; more than I could possibly
cover in a little blog post!

A couple highlights briefly:

I am really happy that Scott Jenson accepted my invite to do a talk at Akademy,
and I thought it was very well received! Has also created a fair bit of buzz
online. Unfortunately did not manage talk much directly — Akademy is hectic and
I lost track of him.. oh well, maybe he'll come again! :)

![Scott Jenson talking about UI/UX](talk-presenter1.webp)

Tracey Clark gave a great talk about QA and testing.. superhero work! It was
great stuff and I hope inspires more people to both submit good bug reports as
well as to help with triaging.

Gerald Senarclens de Grancy told us about using KDE software to teach kids about
computers, linux, programming, and much more. Really interesting stuff. I expect
it makes a huge impact to reach people so young!

Markus Feilner told us the crazy tale of exposing how the Bavarian government
tried to make a €1 billion+ contract with Microsoft without any sort of
consultation, alternatives, bidding, etc.. wild stuff. I'd like to see such
consideration for open source options in Canadian government back home!

Eike Hein told us all about how he's been exploring the great pyramids powered
by KDE technology — wow that is so cool! That honestly sounds like a dream come
true; it had never even occurred to me people like us were doing such things!
I'd really love to hear more details on this. *hint hint* :D

So many other great talks! Only 2 downsides: I couldn't attend them all and had
to catch up on some later (with this year's _excellent_ recordings), and the
schedule was so tightly packed that there was often only 5 minutes between talks
to run to the bathroom; quite the fast pace when trying to keep hydrated! 😵‍💫

## BoFs

Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday were the BoFs (Birds of a Feather) sessions —
smaller and more informal talks/hackathons/brainstorming/etc.

Definitely far too much to cover, but we had good sessions with the Penpot folks
and have some ideas how to help move things forwards there, we have hope for
exciting updates to Bugzilla, we traded and talked about some really cool
stickers... and a laundry list of other cool stuff, tons of work
happening/planned.

## Day trip

On Wednesday the KDE folks took a short breather from the hectic schedule to do
something fun together — visit a fancy Austrian chocolate factory called Zotter!

They started with a short film explaining some of who they are and how they
work, which was _shockingly_ well-produced; it looked and sounded amazing!

I really tried to pace myself because the tour includes apparently 300+ tasting
stations, and even so by the end I was a bit sick of chocolate. 🤯 🍫 😵

It was super interesting though and very tasty. I ended up buying a bunch from
the gift shop to bring home as souvenirs of course! My favorites were the white
chocolates and the ones with coffee; specifically the coffee & dates one is
amazing. 😋

After Zotter we all went to enjoy some food and drinks (no chocolate!), and the
weather was being perfectly accommodating for us!

After we got back to Graz proper several of us ended up sharing some excellent
Fella-brewed liqueur in the hotel lobby, and getting very loud and silly and
generally having a fun evening together to wrap up an absolutely lovely day.
